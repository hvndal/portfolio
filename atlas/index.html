An atlas of 94 conditions

What happens whenthe brain breaks

Most of what we know about the brain was learned by studying what happens when one part stops working. Each condition below is a clue. Click one to see where it lives and what it reveals.

By Herman Hundal

The brain, in three dimensions

Turn it, zoom in, and click a marked region to see every condition linked to it. Open any condition and press See in 3D to fly to it.

How to read it

i.

Every dot on the ring is a condition, grouped and coloured by the system it disrupts.

ii.

Lines run to where damage is most often found. Several lines mean the condition depends on a network.

iii.

The brain is seen from the side, front to the left. Dashed shapes lie deep inside. Click any of them.

The eleven systems

The brain doesn't fail all at once. It fails along the lines of the jobs it does. Pick a system to filter the map.

The brain is not a set of tidy boxes. Most of these conditions come from damage to networks, not single spots, so each line shows where damage is most often reported, not the only cause. The drawing is a schematic, not to scale. This is for curiosity, not diagnosis. Inspired by the Cognitive Disorder Atlas by Lasse Hansen & Lasse Damgaard. Descriptions written fresh from standard neurology references.
