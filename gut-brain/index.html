The gut–brain axis, in DNA

Your gut and your mind share genes

A large genetic study compared four gut diseases with six psychiatric disorders and found 83 stretches of DNA that both sides have in common. Tap a line, a gene or a gut microbe to highlight where it shows up.

How to read it

  • 1q32.1Locus. An address on a chromosome (chromosome 1, long arm, band 32.1) where a variant is linked to both disorders.
  • +Same direction. The variant raises (or lowers) the risk of both. − means it pushes them in opposite directions. 53 of the 83 go the same way.
  • Same causal variant. Colocalisation analysis suggests one single variant drives both signals (posterior probability above 0.7), not two neighbours.
  • Gut microbe link. The same locus also colocalises with the abundance of a group of gut bacteria.
  • GENEDark gene. Highlighted in the paper as active in gut or brain tissue (E-MAGMA). Light genes are other candidates near the locus.
The abbreviations

GERD gastro-oesophageal reflux disease · IBD inflammatory bowel disease · IBS irritable bowel syndrome · PUD peptic ulcer disease
MDD major depression · SCZ schizophrenia · BIP bipolar disorder · ADHD attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder · AN anorexia nervosa · PTSD post-traumatic stress disorder

Source. Gong W, Guo P, Li Y, et al. Role of the Gut-Brain Axis in the Shared Genetic Etiology Between Gastrointestinal Tract Diseases and Psychiatric Disorders: A Genome-Wide Pleiotropic Analysis. JAMA Psychiatry. 2023;80(4):360–370. doi:10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2022.4974
